Did that USDC developer bounty actually pay?

An approved pull request is not automatically a paid invoice. A “$100 bounty” listing, an escrow promise, a green checkmark, and an external settled token transfer are four different kinds of evidence. Here is a seven-step, read-only checklist for Base mainnet Circle USDC.

Safety boundary: This educational guide never connects a wallet, signs anything, scans your transactions, or verifies a payment for you. Always independently inspect authoritative chain receipts. The examples below are fictional; do not paste real sensitive customer data into public tools.

Platforms differ: an escrow-funded bounty can prove funds are deposited without proving that this particular worker has been awarded or paid. Conversely, an unfunded promise does not create escrow.

Seven checks before displaying “Paid”

  1. Same work, real buyer and written acceptance. Match a unique work/order ID to the buyer's actual agreed payment terms. Distinguish “submitted”, “accepted”, “settled” and “paid”. A sponsor's compliment about code quality is not necessarily a payout instruction.
  2. Exact network. Confirm you are looking at Base mainnet, chain ID 8453 (eth_chainId = 0x2105), not Ethereum, Base Sepolia or another L2. The same 0x-shaped wallet address can exist on several networks.
  3. Exact token contract. Native Circle USDC on Base is 0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4C7C32D4f71b54bdA02913. Compare the full address to Circle's official contract list; do not trust a token name, icon, ticker or screenshot alone. USDbC (bridged USDC) is a different asset.
  4. Transaction receipt status. Obtain a real txHash from a trustworthy explorer or authorized read-only RPC. The receipt must exist, correspond to that hash, and have status = 0x1. Pending, reverted and nonexistent transactions do not count.
  5. Actual payee, event and amount. Inspect an ERC-20 Transfer(address,address,uint256) log emitted by Circle's token contract. The decoded to must be your genuine agreed payee; the decoded value must meet the agreed amount. USDC has six decimals: 1 USDC is 1,000,000 atomic units. Beware transfers to a look-alike address, a different recipient, or another token contract.
  6. Finality appropriate to Base. The transaction's block must have reached a finality threshold consistent with your policy (for example the node's finalized chain head). A transaction visible in a preconfirmation feed or merely included in a recent block may not have the same settlement assurance. Do not confuse L2 transaction finality with L1 withdrawal challenge-period terminology.
  7. Independent, unique economic receipt. Count the event by at least (chainId, txHash, logIndex), once across your books. Flag wallet-to-self transfers, refund reversals, the same hash appearing twice in CSV exports, incorrect payment attribution, fees and legal tax implications. Even a valid transfer might be a refund, an internal move or payment for a different customer.

A useful final field: payment_evidence_state = "verified_external_finalized_unique" is an evidence classification, not a guarantee of collectability, ownership or net profit. Preserve the original receipt URL, amount, chain, payee, block, log index and classification reason.

Five mistakes that can erase your margin

  • Working before sponsor approval: “First merged PR wins” contests can pay nothing to every other contributor.
  • Overlooking task bonds: A refundable 1 USDC bond is still capital at risk if your claim expires or is rejected.
  • Ignoring the payout rail: A prize payable via Stripe Connect, Solana USDC, Stellar or a custodial balance is not already native Base USDC in your existing EVM wallet.
  • Confusing a provider's dashboard with independent settlement: A self-reported platform transaction counter can include pending or internal test payments.
  • Booking the same transfer multiple times: A transfer may appear in an explorer, a task dashboard, an emailed receipt and two CSV rows. It remains only one economic event.
